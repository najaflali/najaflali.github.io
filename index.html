📖 What Is fpvdash?

fpvdash is a dashboard made for EdgeTX colour radios. It fills the screen with the flight information that matters most, so you can glance down and understand your quad’s status without hunting through menus. It is built for FPV pilots flying Betaflight quads with ExpressLRS or Crossfire radio links.

The layout is designed to be readable at arm’s length and in bright outdoor light. Large numbers, clear labels and a colour-coded gauge style make it easy to spot a low battery, a weak link or a climbing altitude while you are still flying.

fpvdash also adds practical extras that pilots ask for after a crash or a long flying session: a find-my-quad QR code you can show to anyone nearby, a logbook that keeps a record of your flights, and voice callouts that speak key values out loud so you do not have to stare at the screen.

✨ Key Features

  • Full-screen telemetry view. The whole radio screen becomes your dashboard, with the most important readings front and centre. No tiny widgets squeezed into a corner.
  • Built for Betaflight quads. Reads the telemetry your Betaflight flight controller already sends, so there is no extra hardware to buy.
  • ExpressLRS and Crossfire support. Works with both of the popular long-range radio link systems used by FPV pilots.
  • Find-my-quad QR code. Display a scannable code on the radio screen with your contact details, so a stranger who finds your crashed quad can reach you.
  • Flight logbook. Keeps a running record of your flights so you can look back at where and when you flew.
  • Voice callouts. The radio speaks important values aloud, letting you keep your eyes on the quad instead of the screen.
  • Made for colour radios. Designed from the ground up for EdgeTX radios with colour displays, including popular RadioMaster models.

Step 2 - Save the file to your computer

The page lists the available versions of fpvdash. Pick the newest one at the top of the list, then save the file it offers to a folder you can find again easily, such as your Downloads folder or your Desktop.

Step 3 - Connect your radio to the computer

Plug your radio into your computer with the USB cable that came with it. On the radio, choose the USB storage option when the prompt appears. Your computer will then show the radio’s memory card as a normal removable drive, just like a USB stick.

Step 4 - Copy the files onto the radio

Open the drive that belongs to your radio, then open the folder named SCRIPTS (inside WIDGETS or TOOLS depending on how you plan to launch it). Copy the fpvdash files you saved in Step 2 into that folder. When the copy finishes, eject the drive safely, then unplug the cable.

Step 5 - Open fpvdash on the radio

Power on your radio and your quad. Open the EdgeTX tools or widgets menu, find fpvdash in the list, and launch it. The dashboard will fill the screen and begin reading telemetry as soon as your receiver link is up.

🎛️ Using the Dashboard

Once fpvdash is running, the screen shows your live flight readings in a large, easy-to-scan layout. Values update in real time as your quad flies. Nothing needs to be configured before your first flight, but you can adjust the display to suit how you like to fly.

Use the radio’s physical buttons and scroll wheel to move between screens and change settings. The controls follow the same pattern you already know from EdgeTX, so there is nothing new to learn.

If a reading looks blank on your first flight, it usually means your quad is not yet sending that particular value over the telemetry link. Check that your receiver is bound and that your flight controller is set up to report telemetry, then try again.

📱 Find-My-Quad QR Code

Losing a quad in tall grass or on a wooded hillside is a stressful experience. The find-my-quad feature turns your radio screen into a scannable QR code that holds your contact information.

If someone finds your quad, they can point a phone camera at the radio screen and immediately see how to reach you. Add your details once in the settings, and the code is ready whenever you need it. It is a small feature that can save an expensive model.

📓 Logbook and Voice Callouts

The built-in logbook keeps a record of your flying sessions. Over time it builds into a useful history you can review to see how often you fly and how your sessions compare. It lives on the radio’s memory card alongside the rest of your EdgeTX data.

Voice callouts work alongside the visual dashboard. Instead of reading a number on screen, you hear it spoken through the radio’s speaker or earphones. This is especially handy for values you want to monitor continuously, because it lets you keep your eyes on the quad while still staying informed.

🛠️ Troubleshooting

The radio does not show up on my computer. Try a different USB cable, since some cables only carry power and cannot transfer files. Also make sure you selected the USB storage option on the radio’s screen after plugging in.

fpvdash does not appear in the menu. Double-check that the files landed in the correct folder on the radio’s memory card, then power the radio off and on again so EdgeTX rescans the card.

Some readings stay blank. Confirm your receiver is bound and that your flight controller has telemetry enabled. Values that your quad does not send will simply remain empty until that sensor is active.

The screen looks cramped or cut off. Make sure you are running a recent version from the download page, and check that your radio’s screen resolution matches the version of fpvdash you copied across.
